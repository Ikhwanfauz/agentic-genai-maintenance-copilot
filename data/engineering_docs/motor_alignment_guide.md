# Motor and Pump Alignment Maintenance Guide

Document ID: ENG-MOTOR-001
Revision: 1
Applicable assets: M-101, P-101

## Correlated Pump and Motor Vibration

A pump and its driving motor form a coupled mechanical system. Increasing vibration on both machines may indicate coupling misalignment, soft foot, looseness, foundation issues, pipe strain, or bearing degradation.

Compare timestamps and trend direction across both assets. Correlation supports further inspection but does not prove a single root cause.

## Motor Current and Temperature

Increasing motor current can result from mechanical overload, hydraulic overload, electrical imbalance, voltage conditions, bearing friction, or misalignment. Review motor current together with temperature, vibration, operating load, and electrical measurements.

A gradual increase in current, temperature, and drive-end vibration warrants inspection. Verify electrical supply conditions before attributing the pattern solely to a mechanical fault.

## Alignment Inspection

Before alignment work, apply the approved isolation and lockout procedure. Inspect coupling condition, soft foot, baseplate integrity, hold-down bolts, and pipe strain.

Record initial and final alignment measurements. A work order may recommend alignment inspection, but maintenance execution requires human authorization and site safety controls.
