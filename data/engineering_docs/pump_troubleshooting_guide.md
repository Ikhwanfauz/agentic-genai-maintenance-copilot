# Centrifugal Pump Troubleshooting Guide

Document ID: ENG-PUMP-001
Revision: 1
Applicable assets: P-101, P-102, P-201

## Elevated Vibration

Evaluate vibration as a trend rather than relying on one reading. Confirm the sensor location, unit, timestamp, and data-quality flag before making a maintenance recommendation.

Increasing pump vibration accompanied by bearing-temperature rise may indicate lubrication degradation, bearing wear, soft foot, pipe strain, or coupling misalignment. Compare pump vibration with the driving motor. Correlated increases across the coupled pump and motor strengthen the case for inspecting alignment, mounting, coupling condition, and bearing health.

Recommended checks include visual inspection, abnormal-noise assessment, lubrication review, hold-down bolt inspection, and laser-alignment verification. Do not diagnose a failed bearing from vibration trend alone.

## Reduced Flow and Discharge Pressure

A simultaneous reduction in flow rate and discharge pressure may be associated with suction restriction, impeller wear, internal recirculation, incorrect valve position, reduced driver speed, or process-condition changes.

Verify suction pressure, valve lineup, strainer condition, process demand, and instrument validity before recommending intrusive maintenance. A stable suction pressure with decreasing discharge pressure and flow can justify inspection of pump hydraulic performance.

## Cavitation Indicators

Possible cavitation indicators include unstable vibration, crackling noise, reduced flow, fluctuating discharge pressure, and inadequate suction pressure.

Check suction-system restrictions, liquid level, temperature, vapor-pressure margin, and valve position. Cavitation must not be concluded from vibration alone.
